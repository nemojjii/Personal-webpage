*Placeholder note.*

Perceptual metrics are built from just-noticeable differences — a difference is
felt, therefore a distance exists. Attention seems to work the other way round:
what I notice determines which differences are available to be felt at all.

So the ordering is suspicious. If attention selects the coordinates, then the
metric is not defined on a fixed manifold; it is defined on whatever submanifold
attention has currently chosen, and the "distance" between two states depends on
a choice that is not part of the states.

Two ways out, neither of which I can carry through yet:

1. Treat attention as a projection $\pi: M \to S$ onto a lower-dimensional
   submanifold, and the perceived metric as the pullback $\pi^* g_S$. Then the
   dependence is explicit and the geometry is honest.
2. Give up on a metric and keep only the conformal class — the angles between
   differences rather than their sizes.

The second is weaker and probably closer to true.
