Sum $N$ cosines whose wave vectors are the directions $\theta_j = \pi j / N$ at
equal magnitude. A translation $\mathbf{p} \mapsto \mathbf{p} + \mathbf{v}$ leaves
the sum unchanged only if $\kappa\,\mathbf{e}(\theta_j) \cdot \mathbf{v} \in
2\pi\Z$ for every $j$ — that is, if $\mathbf{v}$ lies in the dual lattice of the
module generated by the $\mathbf{e}(\theta_j)$.

For $N = 2, 3, 4, 6$ that module has rank $2$ and the dual lattice is a genuine
lattice: the field is periodic, and we get the four crystallographic cases.
For $N = 5$ the module has rank $4$ in a plane of dimension $2$, the dual
contains no non-zero vector, and the only translation leaving the field fixed is
the identity.

So the picture has five-fold symmetry about a point and no symmetry under any
translation whatsoever. Which is, in miniature, why quasicrystals were a surprise.
