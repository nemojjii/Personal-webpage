Perception is not a list of measurements. Two colours can differ by the same
physical amount and yet feel differently far apart; two intervals can be equally
spaced in frequency and unequally spaced in hearing. Wherever a *difference* is
felt more strongly in one region than another, there is something that behaves
like a metric — and the natural question is which geometry it belongs to.

## The question

Suppose perceptual states form a smooth manifold $M$, and that the smallest
noticeable difference at a state $p$ defines a positive definite form $g_p$ on
the tangent space $T_pM$. Then perceived dissimilarity between two states is a
length, and the shortest paths are geodesics:

$$
d(p, q) \;=\; \inf_{\gamma:\,p \rightsquigarrow q}\;
\int_0^1 \sqrt{\,g_{\gamma(t)}\!\left(\dot\gamma(t), \dot\gamma(t)\right)}\, \dd t .
$$

<Theorem kind="definition" name="Perceptual metric" number="1">
  A *perceptual metric* on a space of states $M$ is a Riemannian metric $g$ for
  which the just-noticeable difference at every $p \in M$ is realised by the unit
  sphere of $g_p$ in $T_pM$.
</Theorem>

This much is old; Helmholtz and Riemann are both standing behind it. What is not
settled is the second half of the question.

## The part that interests me

An artist choosing a colour, a word, or an interval is not measuring distances —
they are selecting a point from a space of possibilities under constraint. If the
space of perceptual states carries a metric, the space of expressive *choices*
carries something weaker: a partial order, a set of admissible moves, perhaps a
connection rather than a metric.

<Aside>
  The asymmetry is the interesting part: perception compares, expression commits.
</Aside>

So the conjecture to be made precise is not "art is geometry" — it is that the
map from expressive choice to perceptual effect is a smooth map between two
differently-structured spaces, and that what we call style is a property of that
map rather than of either space alone.

## What would have to be true

- A perceptual metric would have to be *measurable* on some small, honest
  domain — a few dimensions of colour or pitch, not "experience".
- The expressive space would need a structure that is not borrowed from the
  perceptual one by assumption.
- The map between them would need to fail to be an isometry in a way that can be
  stated, not merely asserted.
