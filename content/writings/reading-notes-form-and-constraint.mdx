The usual defence of fixed form is that difficulty improves the writer. The more
interesting claim is geometric: a constraint removes directions of motion, and in
doing so it makes some destinations *closer*. A sonnet does not merely forbid —
it shortens the path to certain turns of thought, which in free verse would have
to be arrived at deliberately.

If that is right, then form is not a filter applied after invention but part of
the search itself, and the question of which forms make which thoughts reachable
is a real question rather than a figure of speech.
